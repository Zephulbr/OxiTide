OxiTide 1.1 speaks your language. The whole interface, the phone remote and the tray menu are now available in 23 languages besides English, and three accessibility additions arrive with them: support for Windows contrast themes, spoken track changes for screen readers, and interface zoom.

> OxiTide is an unofficial, third-party project. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or connected to TIDAL. A paid TIDAL subscription is required. OxiTide plays only what your own account can already stream.

## Languages

Deutsch, Français, Español, Italiano, Português (Brasil), Nederlands, Svenska, Norsk bokmål, Dansk, Suomi, Polski, Čeština, Magyar, Українська, Русский, Română, Ελληνικά, Türkçe, Bahasa Indonesia, 日本語, 한국어, 简体中文 and 繁體中文.

- **Pick one** in Settings → Appearance → Language, or leave it on **System** to follow Windows (or your desktop on Linux). The list has a search field: type the language's own name, its name in English or in the current language, or its code. The app switches at once, with no restart (the text on screen erases and retypes itself in the new language; not with reduced motion), and starts in that language next time.
- **Machine-translated.** Every language was drafted by a machine and has not been checked by a native speaker yet. Expect odd words and stiff phrasing in places. If something reads wrong, **Help translate** under the language picker explains how to report it (no files needed) or fix it yourself; corrections are very welcome.
- **Everything follows the language:** menus, dialogs, tooltips, settings search (it matches your language's words and the English ones), screen-reader labels, the tray menu and taskbar buttons, and the phone remote (which follows the phone's own language). Dates, numbers, durations and sorting use the language's conventions ("11. Feb. 2014", "1,1 Mio.", "2014年2月11日").
- **Fonts:** Polish, Czech, Hungarian, Romanian, Turkish, Cyrillic and Greek text now uses the app's own typefaces; Japanese, Korean and Chinese use the system's fonts with the right regional shapes.
- Still in English: messages that come from TIDAL itself (page and shelf titles), technical error details shown under a translated headline, and the Hi-Fi Rack's painted labels.

## Accessibility

- **Windows contrast themes.** With a contrast theme on (Settings → Accessibility → Contrast themes), OxiTide uses the theme's colours throughout: focus rings, the seek and volume bars, toggles, selected and playing rows, the current page and drop targets stay visible, and decorative layers (the cover backdrop, grain, colour washes) step aside. The Hi-Fi Rack stays usable but drops its look.
- **Track changes for screen readers.** When you start a track, skip, or jump in the queue, screen readers announce "Now playing: title by artists". Tracks that follow on their own are not announced, so listening stays quiet.
- **Zoom.** Settings → Appearance → Zoom makes the whole interface 80–150 % of its size, or use Ctrl+= / Ctrl+− / Ctrl+0. It is remembered between sessions; the mini player keeps its size.

## Install

| Platform | File |
|---|---|
| Windows 10/11 (x64) | `OxiTide_1.1.0_x64-setup.exe` (per user, recommended) or the `.msi` (per machine) |
| Linux (x86_64) | `.AppImage` or `.deb`; `PKGBUILD` for Arch (AUR recipe) |

Check downloads against `SHA256SUMS.txt`. An installed 1.0.1 (or any copy since 0.3) offers the update in **Settings → System → Updates**; your settings, queue and history carry over. deb and AUR installs update through the package manager.

## Known limits and Linux

Unchanged from 1.0: Windows builds are not code-signed (SmartScreen: **More info → Run anyway** after checking the hash), the phone remote uses plain HTTP on your local network, and Linux is a work in progress with the known issues listed in the [1.0 release notes](https://github.com/Zephulbr/OxiTide/releases/tag/v1.0.0). Right-to-left languages are not supported yet.
